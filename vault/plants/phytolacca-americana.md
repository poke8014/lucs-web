# Phytolacca americana (American pokeweed)

American pokeweed (*Phytolacca americana*) is a robust herbaceous perennial shrub native to the eastern US (Maine to Florida, west to Texas) but spreading aggressively in California — Central Valley, the coast from Mendocino to San Diego, the Sierra Nevada below 4,000 ft. **All parts of the plant are toxic** (saponins and oxalates) — can be fatally poisonous to humans and livestock when ingested raw. Adds bright **purple-black berries** that are messy, bird-dispersed, and hazardous to curious children. Resprouts annually from a **large fleshy taproot**; aboveground dies back to skeletons after fall frost. Single plant can produce 1,500–7,000 seeds that remain **viable in soil for up to 50 years**. Sometimes eaten in traditional Southern cooking ("poke salad") after **repeated blanching** to remove toxins — preparation matters; raw plant material is dangerous.

Source: UC IPM Pest Note PN 74173 (updated 09/2020). See [[sources/ucipm-residential]].

## Identification

- **Form:** erect herbaceous perennial shrub, **4–10 ft tall, 3–5 ft wide.**
- **Stems (key field ID):** **smooth, stout, purplish, branched extensively, up to 2 in in diameter.** The red stems combined with bright green leaves and dark purple berries are the visual signature.
- **Leaves:** alternate, smooth, elliptic, tapered, **up to 1 ft long and 4–7 in wide**. **Strong unpleasant scent when crushed.**
- **Flowers:** small white-pinkish clusters that hang from branches. 5 sepals, no petals. Erect when blooming, droop as fruits develop. Self-fertile → high fruit set.
- **Berries:** **dull green when immature → glossy purple-black at maturity in late summer.** Distinctive in fall and winter.
- **Taproot:** **large fleshy** — drives the perennial regrowth from year to year.
- **Reproduction:** seed only — 1,500–7,000 per plant per year. **Black lens-shaped glossy seeds, viable up to 50 years in soil.**
- **Bird dispersal:** robins, cedar waxwings, warblers, pigeons all eat the berries. New populations frequently appear below tree canopies, fence rows, and other bird perches.
- **Toxicity:** all parts contain saponins and oxalates — fatally toxic to humans and livestock when ingested raw or improperly prepared. Roots most toxic. **Severe digestive-tract irritation** is the primary symptom. Pokeweed is sometimes eaten as a Southern traditional dish ("poke salad") but **requires repeated blanching** — raw consumption is dangerous, especially for children.

## Remove

Default method: [`dig_taproot`](../synthesis/invasive-removal-methods.md#dig_taproot).

- **Pull young plants by hand** — easy at the seedling stage before the taproot develops. Catch them where birds perch.
- **Dig out established plants with a shovel** — get the large fleshy taproot. Difficult in summer when soils are dry; easier in spring/fall.
- **Bag ripe berries when removing mature plants** — every berry is a packet of long-viable seeds. Don't drop them on the ground or compost them.
- **Cultivation works on new seedlings** in raised beds or tilled areas; **doesn't work on established plants** with their deep root systems.
- **Foliar herbicides:** apply when leaves are fully developed, plant is actively growing, soil moisture is adequate — typically April through August.
  - **Glyphosate 2–3%** (spray-to-wet): good to excellent control. ~2.5–4 oz of a 41% concentrate per gallon of water.
  - **Triclopyr 0.75–1%** (spray-to-wet) — broadleaf-selective, doesn't harm grasses. ~1–1.25 oz of a 61% concentrate per gallon. Mix with seed oil (Hasten-EA) for better penetration on woody stems.
  - For air temperatures over 80°F, **use amine triclopyr or glyphosate** — ester triclopyr is subject to vaporization.
- **Cut-stump treatment** is an alternative for mature plants where foliar isn't practical: cut, then paint the stump within minutes with concentrated glyphosate or triclopyr.
- **Preemergent for areas with seedbank present:** dithiopyr, isoxaben, oryzalin, trifluralin.

## Prevent

- **Monitor below trees, fence rows, and other bird perches** — that's where new pokeweed plants establish. Catch seedlings early before they form the perennial taproot.
- **Bag berries when removing mature plants.** Don't compost — seeds survive home compost and 50 years in soil.
- **Keep curious children away from pokeweed berries** — they're attractive but toxic. Pets are generally less interested but should still be supervised around pokeweed.
- **Don't try to eat pokeweed** unless you're following a tested traditional recipe with multiple blanchings — raw or undercooked plant material is genuinely dangerous.
