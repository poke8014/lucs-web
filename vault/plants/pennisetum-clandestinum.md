# Pennisetum clandestinum (kikuyugrass)

Kikuyugrass (*Pennisetum clandestinum*; also classified as *Cenchrus clandestinus* in recent taxonomy) is an extremely aggressive perennial grass weed of California turf, ornamental beds, orchards, and noncrop areas. Native to East Africa, imported to California around 1918 as an erosion-control ground cover, it spread quickly into a major weed. Forms a network of **thick fleshy stems that root at every node** — both above-ground (stolons) and 1–4 inches deep (rhizomes). Patches expand at ~4 sq ft per month, and a single inch of chopped stem with a node will produce a new plant. **Once kikuyu reaches ~40% of a lawn, UC IPM advises maintaining it as the turf species rather than fighting it** — that's how hard it is to dislodge.

Source: UC IPM Pest Note PN 7458 (updated 08/2011). See [[sources/ucipm-residential]].

## Identify

- **Form:** prostrate perennial grass growing 60–90°F (tolerates to 100°F). In coastal areas may not go dormant; inland often browns out Nov–Feb.
- **Leaves:** light green, 1–10 in long, **flat blades 1/8–1/4 in wide**, **pointed tips**.
- **Confused with St. Augustine grass:** kikuyu has **pointed leaf tips** and **flat slightly-hairy leaves**; St. Augustine has rounded tips and folded/creased hairless blades. UC IPM notes kikuyu has sometimes been mistakenly propagated as St. Augustine.
- **Leaf collar (key field ID):** **long fringe of hairs parallels the stem in the collar region** — diagnostic.
- **Stems:** thick fleshy, form a dense mat or thatch above the soil (stolons) AND a network of rhizomes 1–4 in deep. Each node sprouts roots and shoots.
- **Flowers:** **stimulated by mowing.** Pollen sacs extend above the leaves on slender white filaments — gives infested turf a whitish cast often mistaken for fungus.
- **Seeds:** dark brown, ~1/8 in. Produced in terminal leaf sheaths (hidden from view).
- **Spread:** primarily **stolons and rhizomes**, secondarily by seed. Mowing/cultivation/renovation equipment is the major vector — fragments establish wherever they land in moist soil.

## Remove

Default method: [`dig_rhizome_complete`](../synthesis/invasive-removal-methods.md#dig_rhizome_complete).

- **Catch new patches small.** Once established, kikuyu is one of the hardest weeds in California to dislodge. Hand removal is the primary home-garden tool.
- **Don't cultivate/hoe casually** — chopping rhizomes scatters fragments that re-root, especially if irrigation follows. The hoeing-then-watering sequence is essentially propagation.
- **Mulch:** strong landscape fabric with full edge overlap can work; **organic mulch is generally too permeable** — kikuyu pushes rhizomes through.
- **Solarization** with clear plastic over moist soil for 4–6 weeks in mid-July to mid-September. Works inland; **unlikely to work coastally** because seasonal fog limits the heat.
- **In turf:**
  - **Healthy dense turf** with proper mowing, irrigation, and fertilization slows kikuyu invasion but doesn't eradicate it. Tall fescue varieties reduce kikuyu impact somewhat.
  - **Spot glyphosate** kills isolated plants but takes the surrounding turf with it — overseed open spots immediately.
  - **Postemergent (cool-season turf only):** triclopyr suppresses (multiple apps per year). Don't use on warm-season turf.
  - **Postemergent (warm-season turf):** quinclorac suppresses.
  - **Preemergents** (apply March): bensulide, benefin, dithiopyr, pendimethalin, prodiamine. Home-use options. **Won't kill established plants** — only prevents new seedling germination.
  - **If ≥40% of the lawn is kikuyu:** UC IPM's pragmatic recommendation is to maintain it as the turf species. Otherwise plan **complete renovation** — kill everything with glyphosate, solarize, replant with a competitive turf species.
- **In ornamental beds:** hand-pull and spot-treat. **Sethoxydim and fluazifop** are grass-selective home-gardener options that won't harm broadleaf ornamentals; clethodim is licensed-applicator-only. Wick or sponge applicator with glyphosate where selectivity matters.

## Prevent

- **Clean equipment between sites.** A single rhizome fragment on a mower transports kikuyu to a new yard.
- **Inspect incoming sod, soil, and plants** for kikuyu contamination.
- **Don't irrigate after cultivation** in infested beds — that's the propagation step.
- **Watch ditch banks, fence rows, and the edges of orchards** — kikuyu invades wherever water plus warmth plus disturbed soil meet.
