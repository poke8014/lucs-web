# Trifolium repens (white clover, and related weed clovers)

"Clover" in California yards covers plants in three legume genera: *Trifolium* (white clover, strawberry clover), *Medicago* (black medic, California burclover), and *Melilotus* (white sweetclover, yellow sweetclover). All are Fabaceae with the family's trifoliate leaves and pealike flowers, all fix atmospheric nitrogen via *Rhizobium* root bacteria — which is why a green clover patch on a yellowing lawn signals **low soil nitrogen**. UC IPM's Pest Note treats them together; this page does the same, using **white clover (*Trifolium repens*)** as the primary slug since it's the most-recognized weed-vs-turf clover. California burclover (*Medicago polymorpha*) — with its spiny burs that stick to socks and pet fur — is often the more annoying species to remove from residential yards.

Source: UC IPM Pest Note PN 7490 (updated 10/2007). See [[sources/ucipm-residential]].

## Identification

- **All clovers in this group:** trifoliate compound leaves and "pealike" flowers in heads or clusters; symbiotic *Rhizobium* nodules on the roots fix atmospheric nitrogen.
- **White clover (*Trifolium repens*) — perennial:** **creeping stems root at the nodes**, forming large clumps. Leaves trifoliate with 1/4–1/2 in leaflets. Flowers in white-to-pinkish heads.
- **Strawberry clover (*Trifolium fragiferum*) — perennial:** more robust than white clover, leaflets longer and narrower, pink flower heads less showy.
- **Black medic (*Medicago lupulina*) — annual:** trailing stems from a taproot, three oval-shaped leaflets finely toothed with prominent veins. Small bright yellow flowers in clusters; one seed per small black pod.
- **California burclover (*Medicago polymorpha*) — annual:** similar to black medic but with **spiny burs** that contain several seeds — the burs cling to clothing, socks, and pet fur.
- **Sweetclovers (*Melilotus* spp.) — annual or biennial:** erect, 2–5 ft tall. Leaf margins toothed more than halfway back from the tip. Yellow (*M. officinalis*) or white (*M. alba*) flowers in elongated terminal racemes.
- **Confused with *Oxalis*:** clover leaves have a stipule and no leaflet-tip indentation; *Oxalis* leaflets have a pronounced indentation at the top and lack stipules. Clovers' pea-like flowers vs. *Oxalis* 5-petaled regular flowers.
- **Germination:** fall, when soil temperatures are 50–60°F. Winter rain sustains annuals; perennials need irrigation through dry summers.

## Remove

Default method: [`hand_pull`](../synthesis/invasive-removal-methods.md#hand_pull) for annuals; [`dig_taproot`](../synthesis/invasive-removal-methods.md#dig_taproot) for established perennials.

- **Annual clovers (black medic, burclover, sweetclovers):** hand-pull, hoe, or cultivate before seed set. Easy at the seedling stage; harder once burs form (burclover) or stems lignify (sweetclovers).
- **Perennial clovers (white, strawberry):** seedlings come up easily, but once established the creeping rooted stems form a connected clump — dig it out, glyphosate can suppress but rarely kills cleanly. **Glyphosate at high rates suppresses some clovers** but doesn't eradicate.
- **Mulch** (3–4 in organic, or landscape fabric + bark) blocks seedling germination before they emerge; thicker (4–6 in) layers smother existing seedlings.
- **Composting and solarization are less effective** than for other weeds — the **hard seed coat** of clovers tolerates heat and survives composting. Plan multi-year control because seed germinates over many years.
- **In turf — fertilization shifts the competitive balance:**
  - **Increase nitrogen** to ~1 lb active N per 1,000 sq ft per month of active growth (max 4 lb/year). Yellow turf + green clover signals N deficiency that favors clover.
  - **Reduce phosphorus** — high P promotes clover.
- **Postemergent turf herbicides:**
  - **Cool-season turf (fescue, ryegrass):** triclopyr is effective. Mecoprop and dicamba also work.
  - **Warm-season turf (bermudagrass, zoysiagrass, kikuyugrass):** mecoprop and dicamba; **don't use triclopyr** (damages warm-season grasses).
  - **2,4-D injures but doesn't control clover** — skip it.
- **In ornamental beds:** none of the turf herbicides are safe near desirable broadleaf plants. Rely on hand-pulling + mulch.
- **Pre-emergent for landscape pros:** isoxaben handles annual clovers well around woody shrubs and trees; many annual flowers tolerate it.

## Prevent

- **A green clover patch in a yellow lawn is a fertilizer-balance signal.** Address the underlying nitrogen deficiency before chasing the clover.
- **Bee-sting concern:** clover blooms attract bees — relevant for yards with kids playing barefoot. Mowing flowers off promptly is the simplest mitigation.
- **Burclover burs travel on pet fur and socks** — they're a re-introduction vector once you've cleared a patch. Brush pets after walks through infested neighborhood areas.
- **Sweetclover blooms can be 5 ft tall** in unmowed corners; remove before bolting if you don't want them tall and seedy.
