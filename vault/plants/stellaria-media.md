# Stellaria media (common chickweed)

Common chickweed (*Stellaria media*) is a widespread winter annual (family Caryophyllaceae) found in California gardens, low-maintenance lawns, and agricultural fields. Two related weed chickweeds — sticky chickweed (*Cerastium glomeratum*) and mouseear chickweed (*Cerastium fontanum* ssp. *vulgare*) — are covered by the same UC IPM Pest Note and managed identically. Chickweeds form dense mats of succulent foliage, complete a generation in **5–6 weeks**, and produce ~800 seeds per plant — but the seedbank only persists ~7–8 years, so consistent prevention of seed set substantially depletes infestations. Hand removal works if you catch them before flowering and don't drop fragments on damp soil (stems can re-root from nodes).

Source: UC IPM Pest Note PN 74129 (updated 04/2006). See [[sources/ucipm-residential]].

## Identification and Life Cycle

- **Common chickweed (*Stellaria media*) form:** prostrate or erect, somewhat succulent annual forming a dense mat. 2 in tall in lawns, 4–6 in elsewhere or in shade.
- **Stems (key field ID):** **a diagnostic band of hairs runs along one side of the stem.** Bright green smooth leaves grow opposite each other on each node. Leaves have an obvious pointed tip.
- **Flowers:** small with 5 white petals, but **petals are deeply divided so each flower appears to have 10 petals**.
- **Roots:** shallow and fibrous.
- **Sticky chickweed (*Cerastium glomeratum*):** similar in appearance but more upright. Leaves and stems hairy (often with glandular secretions) giving a gray, sticky feel.
- **Seed:** germinates at or near the soil surface from January to early March in cooler areas when soil temperatures reach about 59°F (optimal 54–68°F). Seeds germinate in large numbers after irrigation or rain. Lifecycle 5–6 weeks. ~800 seeds per plant; seedbank depletes 95% in 7–8 years.
- **Habitat:** prefers neutral-pH soils with high nitrogen and good moisture; performs poorly on acid soils.

## Remove

Default method: [`hand_pull`](../synthesis/invasive-removal-methods.md#hand_pull).

- **Control chickweed before it flowers.** The 5–6 week germination-to-seed cycle gives a narrow window — start monitoring in late fall, remove seedlings whenever you see them.
- **Hand-pull, hoe, or cultivate** while soil is dry and plants are small. If soil is moist and plants are large, **remove plant debris from the site** — stems re-root from nodes in damp ground.
- **Solarization** (clear plastic over moist soil) in late summer kills the seedbank. Don't disturb the soil afterward — that brings up deeper seeds. Most effective in inland areas with hot summers.
- **Mulch (2+ in organic, or landscape fabric + cover mulch)** suppresses germination. Synthetic mulches need bark or rock on top to reduce UV deterioration.
- **In turf:** maintain a thick vigorous lawn; deep + infrequent irrigation. Don't over-apply nitrogen.
- **Preemergent herbicides** (apply in late fall / early winter, before seeds germinate): benefin, dithiopyr, oryzalin, pendimethalin, prodiamine, trifluralin. Multiple home-gardener-available; check label for vegetable-garden compatibility (varies).
- **Postemergent herbicides** in turf: dicamba (broadleaf-selective; harms ornamentals if drift). Triclopyr in cool-season grasses only — **harms warm-season turf** like bermudagrass and kikuyugrass.
- **Nonselective spot spray** (glyphosate, diquat, glufosinate) in beds — keep off desirable plants.

## Prevent

- **The 7–8 year seedbank depletes** if you prevent seed set consistently for several years. Each year of zero seed set has compound effects.
- **No commercial biocontrol** — only grazing by birds (chickweed seeds are actually preferred chicken food — hence the name).
- **Common chickweed hosts insect pests** (lygus, thrips) and **plant viruses** (tomato spotted wilt, cucumber mosaic) — extra reason to keep it out of vegetable gardens.
- **Mature seeds need no dormancy period** — they germinate as soon as they hit moist soil. Removing fruiting plants before seed disperses is the single highest-leverage action.
