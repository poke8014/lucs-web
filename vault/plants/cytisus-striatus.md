# Cytisus striatus (Portuguese broom)

Cytisus striatus (Portuguese broom) is a perennial shrub (family Fabaceae) found in the San Francisco Bay region, Peninsular Ranges and south coast of California. It is commonly found in coastal scrub and grasslands, where it is expected to continue to expand its range. Portuguese broom was introduced as a landscape ornamental, and is often confused with Scotch broom ( Cytisus scoparius). Broom is unpalatable to most livestock, except goats, so it decreases rangeland value while increasing fire hazards. These leguminous plants produce copious amounts of seed, and may resprout from the root crown if cut or grazed. It is native to Spain and Portugal.

## Identify

- **Form:** perennial shrub, similar in habit and size to Scotch broom ([[plants/cytisus-scoparius]]); to ~10 ft.
- **Stems:** angular like Scotch broom (key for separating from leafier French broom).
- **Distinguishing feature vs Scotch broom:** **hairy fruit pods** (hence the common synonym "hairy-fruited broom"). Pods are densely silky-hairy; Scotch broom pods are hairy only along the margins.
- **Flowers:** yellow, pea-shaped, single or paired in leaf axils, like Scotch broom.
- **Range in California:** SF Bay region, Peninsular Ranges, south coast — narrower than the other brooms.
- **Seedbank:** long-lived (treat as decadal, same as Scotch broom).
- **Note:** No species-specific WRIC report. WRIC management notes for Scotch broom (*C. scoparius*) and the [UC IPM Brooms PN](../raw/articles/ucipm-residential/brooms.md) — which explicitly covers *C. striatus* — apply directly.

## Remove

Default method: [`cut_stump_herbicide`](../synthesis/invasive-removal-methods.md#cut_stump_herbicide).

- **Manage identically to Scotch broom.** Both Cal-IPC and UC IPM treat *C. striatus* as a Scotch-broom-class problem with the same mechanical and chemical recipes — see [[plants/cytisus-scoparius]] for the full playbook.
- **Small plants:** hand-pull seedlings; weed wrench for plants up to ~3–4 ft. Extract the entire root.
- **Established shrubs:** cut at ground level + paint cut stump with herbicide within minutes. Cut at the **beginning of the dry season** for energy depletion.
- **Cut-stump herbicide** recipes:
  - Triclopyr 61% (Garlon 4 Ultra) → 1:4 with water.
  - Glyphosate 41% → 1:3 with water.
- **Foliar spray** (active growth, late summer / early fall): triclopyr ester 0.75–1.5% or glyphosate 1.5–2% of 41% concentrate.

## Prevent

- **Multi-year seedling sweeps** for at least a decade — long-lived seedbank dominates the constraint.
- **Soil disturbance triggers the seedbank** — favor cut-stump over digging.
- **Don't confuse with Scotch broom in field IDs** — confirm via the hairy pods. Management is identical, but range and aliases differ enough that record-keeping matters for restoration projects.

## Why it's a problem

**Q1.1:** Soil N content increased somewhat not as nodulated as some other brooms. Can carry fire to the tree canopy.

**Q1.2:** Displaces native species and forms monospecific stands of one mature shurb per two square meters. Native communities are not monospecific
