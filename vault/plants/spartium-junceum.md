# Spartium junceum (Spanish broom)

Spartium junceum(Spanish broom) is a deciduous shrub (family Fabaceae) found throughout the western part of California. Spanish broom was introduced as a landscape ornamental and was planted along highways to prevent soil erosion. It may grow into monospecific stands, excluding native species. Broom is unpalatable to most livestock except goats, so it decreases rangeland value, while increasing fire hazards. These leguminous plants produce copious amounts of seed, and may resprout from the root crown if cut or grazed.

## Identify

- **Form:** rapidly-growing deciduous shrub, **10–15 ft tall** (taller than Scotch / French / Portuguese broom).
- **Stems:** long, smooth, slender, cylindrical, erect, with few branches — **rush-like appearance**. Because leaves are ephemeral, the green stems do most of the photosynthesis.
- **Leaves:** small (0.5–1 in), oval, smooth-margined. Drop after ~4 months — the plant looks leafless most of the year. This is the key field distinction vs the other brooms.
- **Flowers:** large yellow pea-shaped flowers up to 1 in long, in terminal racemes on current-year shoots, on short stalks on both sides of the main stem.
- **Pods:** 1.5–4 in long (longer than other brooms), dark brown when mature, covered with long silky silvery hairs. 10–18 seeds per pod, ejected several feet when pods open.
- **First flowering:** 18 months–3 years of age. Plants live up to 30 years.
- **Seedbank:** up to 30 years viability.
- **Tolerance:** salt-laden coastal winds; temperatures as low as 14°F; urban pollution.

## Remove

Default method: [`cut_stump_herbicide`](../synthesis/invasive-removal-methods.md#cut_stump_herbicide).

- **Hand-pulling:** works only on seedlings and small shrubs — generally not effective once established (same as French broom and gorse).
- **Weed wrench or woody-weed extractor** for larger established plants. Extract the entire root or stump sprouting follows.
- **Established shrubs:** cut at ground level with saws. Cutting alone almost always produces resprouts — combine with cut-stump herbicide or multiple cuts over years.
- **Timing for energy depletion:** cut **at the end of the dry season** to reduce resprouting from the root crown.
- **Cut-stump herbicide** (paint within minutes of cutting):
  - Triclopyr 61% (Garlon 4 Ultra) → 20% v/v in water, or undiluted Garlon 3A, or 50% Garlon 3A in water.
  - Glyphosate 41% → **25–50% v/v in water** (higher rate can reduce resprouting but may exceed label for dense stands).
- **Foliar spray** (active growth, late summer / early fall):
  - Triclopyr ester 0.75–1.5% + surfactant.
  - Glyphosate 1.5–2% v/v of 41% concentrate.
- **What doesn't work:** burning alone (stimulates seedbank germination); grazing alone except with confined goats (broom alkaloids make foliage unpalatable to most other livestock).

## Prevent

- **30-year seedbank** dictates the timeline — annual seedling sweeps for at least a decade.
- **Soil disturbance triggers the seedbank** — minimize disturbance after clearing.
- **Don't burn alone.** Burning can be useful only as part of a sequence with herbicide follow-up.
- **Stems are nearly leafless** — relying on visual scans for resprouts is harder than with leafier brooms. Mark cut stumps in restoration sites so they can be revisited.
