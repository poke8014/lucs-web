# Tribulus terrestris (puncturevine / goathead)

Puncturevine (*Tribulus terrestris*) is a summer annual broadleaf with **spiky seedpods that can puncture bicycle tires, pet paws, and bare feet**. Native to southern Europe; introduced to California where it thrives in hot, dry, disturbed sites that other plants can't tolerate. Forms dense prostrate mats 2–5 ft in diameter. The seedpod splits into five flat **spiny burrs ("caltrops")** that lodge in tires, fur, and clothing — the primary dispersal mechanism. Each plant produces 200–5,000 seeds. **Seed viability is up to 5 years**, so persistent annual removal before seed-set is the long-term strategy. **Two introduced biocontrol weevils** (*Microlarinus lareynii* and *M. lypriformis*) are established in California and provide effective control when working together.

Source: UC IPM Pest Note PN 74128 (updated 03/2006). See [[sources/ucipm-residential]].

## Identification

- **Form:** prostrate summer annual forming a dense mat 2–5 ft in diameter; stems radiate out from a central taproot. **Plant does not root from stems.**
- **Leaves:** hairy, opposite, divided into **4–8 pairs of opposite leaflets**.
- **Flowers:** **yellow, 5-petaled, up to 1/2 in wide**, in leaf axils.
- **Seedpod (the diagnostic feature):** cluster of **5 flat spiny burrs**, each containing up to 5 seeds. As the seedpod matures it turns gray/tan, hardens, and breaks apart so individual spikes can stick into animals and tires. The name **caltrop** is a metal anti-vehicle/anti-horse device with 4 spikes — same idea: when 3 spikes touch the ground, the 4th points up.
- **Common names:** puncturevine (from tire-puncturing), caltrop, goathead.
- **Taproot:** deep — produced rapidly to enable summer drought tolerance.
- **Germination:** spring and summer from previous year's seeds. Needs good soil moisture and warm temperatures to start. Plants flower within 3 weeks of germination; flowering continues all summer.
- **Seed viability:** up to **5 years** in soil. Most seeds dormant the first season.
- **Hazard:** spiky burrs injure livestock, pets, people, and tires. Toxic to sheep — can cause photosensitivity, skin lesions, blindness, and death in young animals. Nitrate poisoning possible in sheep and cattle.

## Remove

Default method: [`hand_pull`](../synthesis/invasive-removal-methods.md#hand_pull) plus burr collection.

- **Pull plants before they produce seed (before or at flowering).** Plants come out cleanly when soil is moist — the deep taproot is younger and softer at flowering than at seed-set.
- **Sweep up dropped burrs after pulling.** A piece of carpet patted against the ground collects burrs that have already fallen. Pet paws and bicycle tires are the main downstream dispersal vectors — clear the ground before declaring victory.
- **Shallow tilling (~1 in)** of seedlings in larger areas works; **don't till deeper** — buries seeds where they survive longer.
- **Mowing doesn't work** — plants grow flat to the ground.
- **Mulch (3+ in organic, or synthetic landscape fabric)** suppresses germination. Caveat: **burrs dropped on top of mulch can establish from the surface** because of the deep taproot — combine mulch with burr cleanup.
- **Biocontrol — two introduced weevils** are effective when working together:
  - **Seed weevil (*Microlarinus lareynii*)** deposits eggs in immature burrs/buds; larvae destroy seeds.
  - **Stem weevil (*Microlarinus lypriformis*)** lays eggs in stems and crown; larvae tunnel in pith.
  - **Use both together for best control.** Most effective when puncturevine is moisture-stressed.
  - Often available from your **county Agricultural Commissioner** for free collection at established release sites — better than buying from suppliers since locally-adapted populations are likelier to survive.
- **Herbicides** (rarely needed for home use):
  - **Preemergent:** oryzalin, benefin, trifluralin — partial control; apply late winter to mid-spring before germination.
  - **Postemergent:** 2,4-D, glyphosate, dicamba on young plants. Smaller is better.
- **Plant competitive species** in disturbed bare patches — puncturevine struggles where desirable plants establish first.

## Prevent

- **Don't bring puncturevine into clean areas** on shoes, bike tires, mower wheels, or pets. Brush pets after walks through known infested areas.
- **Patrol fence lines, gravel parking, driveways, and trails** — disturbed bare ground is exactly the niche puncturevine occupies. Hand-pull or hoe seedlings on sight.
- **Aerate compacted sites and plant competitive vegetation** — puncturevine retreats from well-vegetated soil.
- **5-year seedbank** means persistent monitoring is the long-term play; not a single-season fix.
