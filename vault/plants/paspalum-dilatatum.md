# Paspalum dilatatum (dallisgrass)

Dallisgrass (*Paspalum dilatatum*) is a warm-season perennial bunchgrass introduced to California from South America in the 1800s as a forage plant — now one of the most difficult lawn weeds to eradicate. It forms tough uneven clumps with **short shallow rhizomes** that send up new shoots in concentric rings, grows faster than most desirable turf, and seedheads escape mower blades by growing flat. UC IPM notes that **complete lawn renovation is often more time- and cost-effective** than chasing dallisgrass with herbicides, since most effective postemergent products are licensed-applicator-only and require 3+ applications over 2–3 years.

Source: UC IPM Pest Note PN 7491 (updated 09/2022). See [[sources/ucipm-residential]].

## Identify

- **Form:** warm-season perennial bunchgrass, clumps expand outward by short shallow rhizomes. Center of mature clump often dies leaving a doughnut shape with another plant in the middle.
- **Rhizomes (key field ID):** **short internodes that look like concentric rings** on the rhizome surface — distinguishes from crabgrass (which has none).
- **Leaves:** coarse-textured, gray-green, **wide (1/4–1/2 in)** vs. most lawn grasses. Up to 4–10 in long if unmowed. Prominent midrib. Slightly flattened, sometimes hairy sheath.
- **Membrane (ligule):** ~1/4 in long; no projections (auricles) — just a few hairs.
- **Stem bases:** frequently purple-tinged.
- **Inflorescence:** flowering stalk 1–5 ft tall with 2–10 alternately arranged drooping branches near the top. Each branch carries 2–4 rows of flat egg-shaped seeds with a fringe of hairs along the entire length.
- **Spread:** seeds (water, mowers, foot/pet traffic) germinate spring/summer at 60–65°F. New shoots also emerge from rhizomes of previous years' plants.
- **Habitat:** wet areas — drainage ditches, low spots, heavily irrigated turf. Tolerates sandy or heavy clay. Once established, drought- and frost-tolerant; less likely to brown out in winter than bermudagrass.

## Remove

Default method: [`dig_rhizome_complete`](../synthesis/invasive-removal-methods.md#dig_rhizome_complete) for individual clumps; full lawn renovation for severe infestations.

- **Dig out young plants before they form rhizomes or seed.** Mature plants also dig out, but **rhizome fragments left behind regrow** — dig generously around the visible clump.
- **Mowing doesn't control dallisgrass** — flowering stalks grow flat enough to escape mower blades; the rhizome network keeps regenerating.
- **Maintain a competitive turf** at optimum mowing height with consistent irrigation. Dallisgrass invades low-maintenance turf with infrequent deep watering and bare patches.
- **In ornamental beds:** dig out plants, remove every rhizome, then cover with thick mulch. **Organic mulch alone doesn't kill mature plants** — combine with mechanical removal first.
- **Solarization** with clear plastic in summer significantly reduces seed and rhizome regrowth — useful before replanting.
- **Preemergents** (home-gardener available for turf and landscapes): benefin/oryzalin, dithiopyr (Dimension), pendimethalin (Scotts Halts), prodiamine. Apply in late winter / early spring before seedlings emerge; irrigate in with ~1/2 in water.
- **Selective postemergent herbicides** (warm-season turf only, **licensed-applicator only**): foramsulfuron (Revolver), thiencarbazone (Celsius), sulfosulfuron (Certainty), trifloxysulfuron (Monument). Need **3 applications** starting in fall when air drops to ~70°F, then 4–6 weeks later, then mid-late spring. MSMA was the gold standard but is now restricted to golf courses / sod farms.
- **Cool-season turf:** essentially no selective option — fluazifop (Fusilade II) on fine fescue and tall fescue only. Multiple monthly applications, avoid hot summer treatments.
- **Spot glyphosate** is the homeowner option: kills dallisgrass plus surrounding turf, leaving bare spots that need overseeding. Often the fastest and cheapest practical control. For severe infestations, complete renovation is more efficient than spot-treating dozens of clumps.

## Prevent

- **Clean mowers between sites** — seed travels readily on equipment, the most common introduction vector.
- **Inspect sod before delivery** for dallisgrass plants or seed contamination.
- **Don't use soil from infested sites** to fill low or bare lawn spots.
- **In infested areas, delay or minimize spring aeration** — small open holes are where new seedlings establish.
