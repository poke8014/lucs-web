# Kyllinga brevifolia (green kyllinga)

Green kyllinga (*Kyllinga brevifolia*) is a weedy sedge — not a grass — that's become a major California turf and ornamental weed over the past 15–20 years. Native to Asia. Forms a network of rhizomes that **expands at over 1 inch per day** in warm weather, sending up new leaves at every node. Often confused with yellow or purple nutsedge, but kyllinga grows in **continuously enlarging patches** (sedge mat-form) rather than isolated plants, has finer leaf blades, and produces a **distinctive small round green "pineapple-shaped" seedhead** rather than the open spikelets of nutsedge. **Hoeing and tilling fragment the rhizomes and transplant them** to new spots — particularly when followed by irrigation, the standard amplification pattern for vegetative spreaders.

Source: UC IPM Pest Note PN 7459 (updated 12/2011). See [[sources/ucipm-residential]].

## Identify

- **Form:** perennial weedy sedge. Best growth in moist or wet sites in full sun; tolerates some shade and drying once established. Active April–October.
- **Height:** ~15 in unmowed; survives mowing as short as 3/4 inch.
- **Color/texture (key field ID):** **finer-bladed than nutsedge**; leaves narrow, 1–5+ in long, emerging from rhizomes as temperatures rise in spring. Doesn't brown out in winter — stays yellow/green when dormant.
- **Seedhead (the diagnostic feature):** **small round green globular flower head ~3/8 in diameter**, looking like a tiny green pineapple. Triangular cross-section stalk 2–8 in long. **Three leaves radiate out from below the flower head.** Distinguishes from nutsedges, which have open spikelets and tubers underground.
- **Below-ground (vs. nutsedge):** kyllinga has **rhizomes, no tubers**. Nutsedges have tubers ("nutlets") at the ends of the roots — easy way to tell after digging up a plant.
- **Reproduction:** rhizomes (rapid lateral spread) AND seeds (~30–75 spikelets per flowerhead, each producing 1 seed; mature plants make 100+ flowers per season, up to 5,000 seeds). Seeds germinate at or very near the surface when soil reaches ~65°F. **Chopped rhizome sections regenerate from each node.**
- **Habitat:** wet areas in turf and landscapes — overwatered lawns, ditch banks, ornamental beds. Most common in bermudagrass turf but found in cool-season turf too.

## Remove

Default method: [`dig_rhizome_complete`](../synthesis/invasive-removal-methods.md#dig_rhizome_complete).

- **Catch new patches small and grub them out** — dig the entire plant including all rhizomes. Monitor for several months afterward to ensure no regrowth.
- **Don't casually hoe or cultivate** — chopping breaks rhizomes into smaller pieces that re-establish. Hoeing followed by irrigation is the worst combination.
- **Fix overwatering** — wet conditions favor kyllinga. Reduce irrigation or improve drainage in low spots. Don't overwater.
- **Mulch in beds:** landscape fabric with full overlap blocks light to the soil; cover with bark/wood chips to reduce UV breakdown. **Organic mulch alone is generally not effective** — kyllinga rhizomes push through.
- **In turf — herbicide options:**
  - **Preemergent** (apply spring before soil reaches 60°F): benefin, bensulide, dithiopyr, pendimethalin, prodiamine. Limits seed germination but won't affect established rhizomes.
  - **Postemergent** for established plants: **halosulfuron in two sequential applications** (when plants show signs of recovering after the first) — most effective approach per UC IPM. Imazosulfuron and trifloxysulfuron are alternatives in some warm-season turf. **MSMA** previously used but now severely restricted.
  - **Spot glyphosate** kills isolated plants but also the surrounding turf — leaves bare spots that need overseeding.
- **In ornamental beds:** halosulfuron is registered for use. Spot glyphosate works but kills desirables on contact. Use a wick applicator for selectivity.
- **For complete renovation:** broadcast glyphosate, wait for full kill, replant with new turf or ornamental.

## Prevent

- **Clean mowers and cultivation equipment** before moving between sites — the primary kyllinga spread vector is contaminated equipment.
- **Don't overwater turf or beds in summer** — kyllinga's competitive advantage is in wet warm conditions. Adjust irrigation to slow invasion.
- **Maintain dense, vigorous turf and ornamental plantings** that shade the soil — seedling establishment fails under thick competition.
- **Once kyllinga reaches ~40% of turf**, eradication is impractical and **complete renovation** is the path forward.
