# Poa annua (annual bluegrass)

Annual bluegrass (*Poa annua*) is one of the most common turfgrass weeds in California — a cool-season grass that germinates in late summer / fall and seeds prolifically through spring. Native to Europe and now worldwide. Each small plant produces about 100 seeds in as few as **8 weeks**, and viable seed forms within days of pollination, so the plant reseeds even in frequently mowed turf. It's the canonical lawn weed for homeowners who want a clean monoculture grass — but as a residential weed in ornamental beds or paths, it's usually managed by mulch + spot removal rather than by herbicide.

Source: UC IPM Pest Note PN 7464 (updated 05/2012). See [[sources/ucipm-residential]].

## Identify

- **Form:** annual or short-lived perennial cool-season grass, 6–8 in tall when unmowed. Survives mowing as short as <1 in — distinguishes from Kentucky bluegrass and rough bluegrass.
- **Two biotypes:** *P. annua* var. *annua* (upright, true annual, more seed); *P. annua* var. *reptans* (lower-growing perennial). Both can occur together.
- **Leaves:** 1–3 in long, light green, **typical *Poa* boat-shaped tip** — leaf tip curves up like the bow of a boat. **Crinkled part way down** the blade is a key diagnostic feature.
- **Stems:** light green, flattened, bent at the base, often rooted at the lower joint.
- **Inflorescence:** branched panicle of seed clusters 1–4 in long. Seedheads form as early as 6 weeks from germination; mostly in spring.
- **Seeds:** amber, ~1/16 in. Each plant produces ~100 seeds.
- **Roots:** weak and shallow — needs frequent water to survive. Thrives in compacted soil and moist partial shade to full sun.

## Remove

Default method: [`hand_pull`](../synthesis/invasive-removal-methods.md#hand_pull).

- **Pull or hoe solitary plants before they seed.** Open spots after removal will quickly re-flush from the seedbank, so monitor and remove repeatedly.
- **Mulch (2–3 in organic, or landscape fabric + bark)** suppresses germination — seeds need light. Plants that germinate *in* the mulch can be easily plucked.
- **Maintain dense competitive turf** — annual bluegrass invades thin or overwatered lawns. Switch to **deep, infrequent irrigation** to disadvantage its shallow root system. Avoid summer fertilization and aeration during fall germination peaks.
- **Preemergent herbicides** (applied a few weeks before fall germination starts, when soil temperatures drop below 70°F): benefin, bensulide, dithiopyr, oryzalin, oxadiazon, pendimethalin, prodiamine. Several home-gardener-available; some lawn-only or ornamental-only — check the label.
- **Postemergent options are limited.** Most that work are licensed-applicator-only and warm-season-turf only (foramsulfuron, sulfosulfuron, trifloxysulfuron, pronamide). Clethodim is grass-selective and works in ornamentals but is licensed-applicator-only.
- **Nonselective spot spray** (glyphosate, diquat, glufosinate, nonanoic acid) is the homeowner option in beds and paths where collateral damage to desirable grass is acceptable.
- **In severe turf infestations**, complete renovation (kill everything with glyphosate, replant during late spring / summer with a competitive turf species, follow up with fall preemergent) is often more effective than chasing the weed.

## Prevent

- **Clean mowers, string trimmers, aerators between sites** — equipment is a major seed-spread vector.
- **Don't overwater shaded areas** — annual bluegrass thrives there. Deep + infrequent watering and avoiding soil compaction shift the competitive balance back to desirable grass.
- **Skip fertilization during fall germination peak** — fertilizing then encourages the annual bluegrass flush.
- **Coastal / cool-summer areas:** annual bluegrass persists year-round. In warmer inland areas it usually dies in summer heat — letting you reseed clean turf into the gap.
