# Cynodon dactylon (Bermuda grass)

Cynodon dactylon (Bermuda grass) is a low-statured, creeping, warm season perennial grass (family Poaceae) that is commonly planted in garden lawns and as turf. It is native to East Africa but has become a cosmopolitan weed in warmer regions around the world, where it tolerates a broad range of soil types and moister levels. Bermuda grass is distinguished by having slightly flattened stems, hairs that jut out at the leaf blade and sheath junctures, grey-green foliage, and an inflorescence comprised of a cluster of 2-6 spikes arranged radially from a single point. Plants reproduce both via rhizomes and seeds. Bermuda grass is fast growing and can out-compete native species, particularly in riparian areas.

## Identify

- **Form:** warm-climate perennial grass with **extensive creeping rhizomes and stolons**. Prostrate by default; can grow to 1.5 ft in shade.
- **Stems / stolons:** slender, tough, scaly, **producing roots at every node**. Stolons run across the soil surface; rhizomes run below.
- **Rhizome depth:** most in **top 2 inches** of soil; some can extend >1 ft deep. This shallow concentration matters for control (solarization works).
- **Leaves:** flat, flexible blades, usually <2.5 in long. **Long hairs around the collar region**, especially at the margins (field ID detail).
- **Inflorescence:** umbel-like, with usually **4–8 spike-like branches** radiating from a central point at the stem tip. The "fingered" inflorescence is distinctive.
- **Hardiness:** rhizomes survive considerable dehydration and drought; **do not survive prolonged freezing** or extended sun exposure. Aboveground growth turns brown below freezing or with day lengths shortening; **rhizomes go dormant below ~64°F (18°C) soil temperature**.
- **Reproduction:** vegetatively from rhizomes and stolons; also by seed. Rhizome and stolon fragments disperse with soil movement, equipment, livestock feed.
- **Seedbank:** some seeds survive **3–4 years**; most germinate within 2 years.
- **Hazards:** contact dermatitis in sensitive people; pollen is a common allergen.
- **Used as:** common turfgrass and forage in warm-temperate regions — also why this species is often deliberately planted as well as a weed.

## Remove

Default method: [`dig_rhizome_complete`](../synthesis/invasive-removal-methods.md#dig_rhizome_complete).

- **Persistent manual removal** of rhizomes and stolons can eliminate Bermuda grass from small areas. Every fragment must come out — the species is the canonical "fragments-regenerate" problem.
- **Tilling or disking** to expose rhizomes to **sun-drying or freezing** is effective. **Apply NO water during drying** — moisture saves the rhizomes. **Do not cultivate moist soil** because rhizome fragments will simply grow in place.
- **Tilling does not kill seeds** — combine with subsequent weed-seedling control.
- **Mow at high setting (2–3 in)** to suppress relative to other turfgrasses (this is the opposite of typical lawn-care; tall mowing in turf shifts the competitive balance against Bermuda grass).
- **Solarization** (clear plastic over moist soil, summer, 6+ weeks) can control small infestations — this works because rhizomes are concentrated in the top 2 in, within reach of the heat. See [`solarize_summer`](../synthesis/invasive-removal-methods.md#solarize_summer).
- **Shading** by other plants, mulches, or landscape fabric suppresses growth (Bermuda grass grows poorly in shade).
- **Foliar herbicides:**
  - **Glyphosate** 1–2 qt/acre or 2% v/v spot, late spring through summer when plants are growing rapidly. **Don't mow for 2–3 weeks before** application (more leaf area = better uptake). Cultivate 7 days after to mix in the herbicide-treated material.
  - **Clethodim**, **fluazifop**, **sethoxydim** are grass-selective alternatives that won't harm broadleaf species. Apply postemergence to seedlings or small regrowth.
- **Clean equipment** after mowing or working in infested areas — single rhizome fragments establish.
- **No biocontrol** — Bermuda grass is too valuable as a turfgrass for a biocontrol program to be developed.

## Prevent

- **Manage soil moisture during removal.** The single biggest mistake is tilling, then watering or expecting rain — that converts a removal pass into a propagation pass.
- **Bermuda grass invades dormant areas in winter.** Maintaining ground cover or fast-establishing competitive vegetation during fall is the simplest prevention.
- **Don't import topsoil from infested sites** — rhizome fragments hitchhike easily.
- **Existing Bermuda grass lawns** are functionally permanent unless solarized, smothered, or repeatedly herbicide-treated; budget for multi-year conversion if reclaiming a Bermuda lawn for native planting.

## Why it's a problem

**Q1.1:** C. dactyon is considered a potent allelopathic plant. In Arizona, C. dactylon increases substrate stability during floods. Can reduce soil nutrient levels and block light penetration to soil surface.

**Q1.2:** C. dactylon forms large dense ground cover mats that can inhibit native flora survival by increasing the density and depth at ground level, thus fragmenting habitat. C. dactylon has been identified as posing a threat to temperate grasslands in North America. Riparian communities in Arizona are severely degraded by C. dactylon. A single shoot from a rhizome may cover 2.5 m2 of soil surface in 150 days after emergence.
