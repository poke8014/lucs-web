# Galium aparine (catchweed bedstraw / cleavers)

Catchweed bedstraw (*Galium aparine*) is an annual (family Rubiaceae — same family as coffee) commonly called cleavers, stickywilly, or "velcro plant" because the **fine downward-curved hook hairs** on stems, leaves, and seed capsules let it cling to clothing, pet fur, and other plants. Stems sprawl up to 6 ft, often tangling into mats. It's a winter/summer annual that **peaks germinating in mid-to-late December** and again in February–March; once it climbs into shrubs or vegetable beds it can smother small plants under its weight. Hand-pulling when soil is damp is highly effective if done before flowering.

Source: UC IPM Pest Note PN 74154 (updated 11/2010). See [[sources/ucipm-residential]].

## Identification

- **Cotyledons (seed leaves):** oblong-to-egg-shaped with slightly notched tips, smooth, hairless, 1/2 to 1 in long.
- **Mature stems:** **up to 6 ft long, square in profile** (especially near the tips). Often form dense tangled mats sprawling on the ground or over vegetation.
- **Leaves (key field ID):** **whorled around the stem (usually 6 or 8 leaves per whorl)**. Small **downward-curved prickles cover both stem and leaves** — these hooks are what cling to clothing and fur, and they aid seed dispersal.
- **Flowers:** small, 4-parted, white-to-greenish-white, borne on short branches from leaf axils.
- **Fruit:** two-lobed spherical-to-kidney-shaped, separates into two nutlets in summer/fall. Each fruit has hooked hairs that aid dispersal. Plants produce 100–400 seeds typically; occasional plants produce 3,000+.
- **Seed viability:** ~3 years in soil.
- **Germination:** seeds germinate at or near the surface — emerges even from 3 in deep in loose soil. **Exposure to light inhibits germination**, so surface-dropped seeds won't sprout until buried.
- **Habitat:** prefers clay/loam soils with high N and P. Tolerates freezing temperatures while vegetative. Best germination in cool moist soil; established plants tolerate dry conditions.
- **Lifecycle:** flowering as little as **8 weeks after germination**. Self-pollinating. Seed-set late spring to mid-summer; plants senesce by summer-fall.

## Remove

Default method: [`hand_pull`](../synthesis/invasive-removal-methods.md#hand_pull).

- **Hand-pull or hoe when soil is damp**, in early spring when plants are still small. Comes up easily before stems begin to sprawl.
- **Remove before flowering** to prevent seed set — short ~3-year seedbank means consistent prevention substantially knocks down infestations.
- **Don't cut to 2–3 in expecting that to control it** — UC IPM notes that cutting at that height actually *increases* biomass production up to 30% vs. uncut plants. Pull fully or leave alone.
- **Mulch (bark, wood chips, leaf litter, gravel, weed tarps)** reduces seedling emergence and makes pulled-plant cleanup easier.
- **In parks or non-agricultural areas:** sow a competitive grass + wildflower mix to suppress bedstraw.
- **Preemergent herbicide** (oryzalin) gives fair control on small areas.
- **Postemergent herbicides:**
  - **Home-gardener:** glyphosate, 2,4-D (partial), dicamba (partial), MCPA (partial). Clove-oil-based organic herbicides (Matratec, BurnOut) kill young plants if applied early.
  - **Licensed only:** oxyfluorfen, quinclorac, diclobenil, carfentrazone.
- **No biocontrol** — economic damage isn't great enough to warrant a program.

## Prevent

- **Brush pets after walks** through bedstraw-infested areas — the hook hairs are how seeds reach a clean yard.
- **Clean clothing and equipment** between bedstraw-infested and clean sites.
- **Catch seedlings early** in fall and again in late winter — those are the two germination peaks.
- **Bedstraw is a foraging plant** in traditional uses (roasted seeds substitute for coffee, young leaves brewed as tea, roots produce red dye) — relevant only if you're actively maintaining a small patch; otherwise removal is the standard call.
