# Malva parviflora (little mallow / cheeseweed)

Little mallow (*Malva parviflora*) and its near-relative common mallow (*Malva neglecta*) are weedy annuals (family Malvaceae) found throughout California in vegetable gardens, ornamental beds, lawns, orchards, and disturbed soil. The family includes cotton, hibiscus, okra, and the original marshmallow plant (*Althaea officinalis*). The two California weed mallows share enough biology and management that **UC IPM's Pest Note covers them together** — this page does the same; differentiating *M. parviflora* from *M. neglecta* matters more to botanists than gardeners. The key management constraint is a deep taproot that becomes woody fast, so any window to remove them by hand closes within a few weeks of germination.

Source: UC IPM Pest Note PN 74127 (updated 03/2006). See [[sources/ucipm-residential]].

## Identification and Life Cycle

- **Form:** annual or short-lived winter annual / biennial. Little mallow (*M. parviflora*) has a more upright growth habit, up to **5 ft tall**, bushlike. Common mallow (*M. neglecta*) tends to be more prostrate, to ~2 ft tall.
- **Seedling leaves (cotyledons):** heart-shaped with smooth edges.
- **First true leaves:** nearly circular or heart-shaped with the notch at the leaf-petiole junction. Slightly crinkled appearance. 5–7 rounded lobes.
- **Mature leaves:** alternate, larger, otherwise similar to the juvenile leaves.
- **Flowers:** white-to-light-pink-to-light-purple, in leaf axils. 5 petals, each notched at the tip (looks like 10). **Petal length is the field separator between species:** *M. neglecta* petals are about 2× the sepal length; *M. parviflora* petals are about the same length as the sepals.
- **Fruit (where "cheeseweed" comes from):** round, smooth-or-wrinkled, flattened buttonlike structure that **looks like a tiny green wheel of cheese**. 10–12 sections per fruit, one seed per section. *M. neglecta* fruit is smooth; *M. parviflora* is wrinkled — the other reliable species separator.
- **Taproot (the management constraint):** single deep taproot that **becomes woody and very difficult to cut or pull** as the plant matures.
- **Seed:** tough impermeable seed coat → low germination each year but **long viability**. Nicked seeds can germinate the same season as they mature. Germination starts with fall rains.
- **Bloom:** little mallow flowers March–September; mature seeds appear **only 15 days after flowering** based on Australian studies — fast turnaround from flower to seed crop.

## Remove

Default method: [`hand_pull`](../synthesis/invasive-removal-methods.md#hand_pull) — but **only while plants are young**.

- **Pull or hoe while plants have four or fewer true leaves.** Once the taproot becomes woody (within weeks of germination) hand removal gets much harder.
- **Cutting off at the crown** can kill young mallow, but older plants resprout from buds below the cut height.
- **Mowing doesn't work** — plants have viable buds on stems below the mower blade. Common mallow's prostrate habit makes it especially mower-resistant.
- **Shallow cultivation** with a cultivator set to **pull plants from the ground or sever the taproot below the soil line** is effective when plants are young and numerous.
- **Mulch (3+ in of bark or wood chips)** suppresses germination and seedling emergence — has to be maintained at depth as it breaks down.
- **Solarization and flaming don't work** for mallows per UC IPM — the seedbank and root tolerance defeat both.
- **What doesn't work chemically:**
  - **Glyphosate is ineffective on mallow** — one of the few weeds it doesn't control. This is a notable WRIC-style "what doesn't work" warning to surface in user-facing copy.
  - **No effective home-use herbicide** is registered for mallow control in the residential setting per UC IPM.
- **What partially works:**
  - **2,4-D** on young plants — turf only, will injure broadleaf ornamentals. Only partial control.
  - **Oryzalin** or **pendimethalin** preemergent in agricultural use; difficult to time because mallow germination is erratic.
- **Biological control:** no commercial biocontrols available.

## Prevent

- **Catch new seedlings within the first month** — that's the entire window for easy mechanical removal. After the taproot lignifies, you're committed to either hand-digging the deep root or living with the plant.
- **Reduce seed set in the soil for years to come** — even pulled plants can release viable seed if they had flowered (15 days from flower to mature seed). Remove plants before flowering, or bag flowering plants.
- **Establish dense competitive plantings** — mallow seedlings struggle under shade.
- **Don't expect glyphosate to fix this** — plan mechanical removal as the primary tactic in residential settings.
