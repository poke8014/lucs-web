# Acer negundo (Box Elder)

Acer negundo (Box Elder) is a California-native tree of Chaparral, Forest, Grassland, Wetland/Riparian, Woodland. Calscape records **87** butterfly/moth species supported. _Structured data only — full description and named host species pending the Calscape page-scrape enrichment pass (see [[sources/calscape]])._

## Identify

- **Form:** Upright
- **Height:** 35 - 66 ft
- **Width:** 40 ft
- **Flower color:** White, Cream
- **Flowering season:** Spring, Winter
- **Seasonality:** Winter Deciduous

## Grow

- **Sun:** Partial Shade, Full Sun
- **Water:** Moderate
- **Summer irrigation:** Max 1x / week once established
- **Soil drainage:** Medium, Slow
- **Soil pH:** 5.0 - 8.0
- **Soil notes:** Tolerates a variety of soils as long as adequate moisture is present
- **Ease of care:** Moderate
- **Propagation:** For propagating by seed:  Scarification and 2-3 mos. stratification; use fresh seeds.
- **Tips:** Plant in sandy, loamy or clay soils that are at least 40 inches deep, with a pH of 5-7.8. It is moderately tolerant of anaerobic soils, tolerant of calcareous soils, and requires only moderately fertile soils. Acer negundo is extremely drought tolerant. It requires a minimum of 15 inches of water per year, and a maximum of 75 inches of water per year once established. Requires moist soil. It is very tolerant of shade. Prefers part shade. Likes stream edges and wet soils.
