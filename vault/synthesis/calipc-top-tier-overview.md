# Cal-IPC Top-Tier Invasive Inventory — Patterns and Takeaways

Cross-cutting synthesis across the 137 California Invasive Plant Council top-tier invasive plants ingested 2026-05-08. See [[sources/calipc]] for what we ingested and how. This page surfaces patterns that don't live on any single plant page.

## Headline numbers

- **137 plants** total (44 rated High, 93 rated Moderate)
- **115 plants** include Luc's [[regions/central-west]] Jepson region
- **86 plants** are *foothills-relevant* — listed for Central West **and** at least one inland habitat (chaparral, valley/foothill grassland, cismontane woodland, riparian woodland/forest, broadleaved upland forest, lower montane coniferous forest). This is the subset that actually applies to a San Jose foothills yard.

## Foothills-priority plants for Phase 1 cleanup

Of the 86 foothills-relevant plants, these are the **worst-of-worst** by Cal-IPC scoring (Impact A / Invasiveness A / Distribution A — only 9 plants in the entire 137 hit this triple-A). All present in Central West with inland habitat fit:

- [[plants/centaurea-solstitialis]] — yellow starthistle. The poster child for CA grassland invasion.
- [[plants/hedera-helix]] — English ivy. Forms dense ground and tree-climbing monocultures.
- [[plants/rubus-armeniacus]] — Himalayan blackberry. Aggressive thicket-former.
- [[plants/cortaderia-jubata]] — jubatagrass.
- [[plants/cortaderia-selloana]] — pampas grass.
- [[plants/bromus-tectorum]] — cheatgrass. Major fire-cycle changer.

Also rated **Cal-IPC High** and confirmed in foothill habitats — all worth identifying in the yard:

- The brooms: [[plants/cytisus-scoparius]] (Scotch), [[plants/genista-monspessulana]] (French), [[plants/spartium-junceum]] (Spanish), [[plants/ulex-europaeus]] (gorse)
- [[plants/centaurea-stoebe]] — spotted knapweed (and 6 other *Centaurea* species in the inventory)
- [[plants/arundo-donax]] — giant reed (riparian)
- [[plants/aegilops-triuncialis]] — barb goatgrass
- [[plants/lythrum-salicaria]] — purple loosestrife (riparian/wetland)

Per Luc's prior knowledge, common foothills weeds named were brooms, blackberry, ivy, fennel — **all are present and rated High (brooms/ivy/blackberry) or Moderate (fennel — [[plants/foeniculum-vulgare]])**.

## Pattern 1: most invasives spread by seed *and* resprout

Across 137 plants:

| Mechanism (Worksheet A "Yes" answers) | Count | % |
|---|---|---|
| Produces seed every year | 118 | 86% |
| Reaches reproductive maturity in ≤2 years | 102 | 74% |
| Produces >1,000 viable seeds per m² | 101 | 74% |
| **Resprouts when cut, grazed, or burned** | **100** | **73%** |
| Has quickly-spreading vegetative structures | 67 | 49% |
| Long-lived seedbank (≥3 years) | 59 | 43% |
| Fragments easily and re-establishes | 44 | 32% |

**The takeaway for management:** mowing or cutting is rarely sufficient — three-quarters of these plants resprout. Most also have a seedbank that outlasts a single growing season. Removal is a multi-year commitment, not a one-time event. See [[concepts/vegetative-spread]] for why.

## Pattern 2: human-mediated dispersal is everywhere

Looking at Q2.5 (human-caused) + Q2.6 (natural) dispersal text:

- **58 plants** (42%) spread on vehicles, equipment, or tires
- **34 plants** (25%) explicitly cite the **horticultural / nursery / landscaping** pathway
- 20 plants are dispersed by livestock; 8 by water; 8 by wind; 6 in contaminated hay/seed/gravel

The horticultural pathway alone accounts for a quarter of the inventory. See [[concepts/horticultural-introduction-pathway]]. Verify before planting; buy from native-focused nurseries.

## Pattern 3: coastal habitats lead, but foothills are heavily affected

Top 10 habitats invaded by count of plants:

| Habitat | Plants invading |
|---------|-----------------|
| coastal prairie | 60 |
| coastal scrub | 60 |
| **riparian woodland** | **53** |
| **valley/foothill grassland** | **46** |
| riparian forest | 39 |
| **chaparral** | **36** |
| **cismontane woodland** | **33** |
| marsh and swamp | 31 |
| coastal bluff scrub | 30 |
| north coast coniferous forest | 29 |

Coastal habitats lead the count, but four of the top eight are inland habitats relevant to a Bay Area foothills yard. Riparian habitats (creeks, seasonal drainages) are particularly susceptible — the South Bay foothills have many of these.

## Pattern 4: PAF score distribution

Most-common score fingerprints (Impact / Invasiveness / Distribution):

| Fingerprint | Count | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| B/B/B | 40 | Median invasive — moderate on all axes |
| B/B/A | 16 | Less impact, but everywhere |
| B/B/C | 11 | Moderate impact, locally limited |
| A/A/B | 10 | Severe & spreading, distribution catching up |
| **A/A/A** | **9** | **Worst possible — bad on all axes** |
| B/B/D | 8 | Moderate impact, very localized |

The 9 triple-A plants are the highest-priority targets statewide; the foothills-relevant subset of those (Hedera helix, Rubus armeniacus, Centaurea solstitialis, etc.) are listed above.

## Pattern 5: a few genera dominate

Top genera by count of invasive species in our set:

| Genus | Species count | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ***Centaurea*** | 7 | Knapweeds and starthistles. Notoriously aggressive grassland invaders. |
| ***Tamarix*** | 4 | Saltcedars. Riparian / desert wash specialists. |
| ***Bromus*** | 3 | Annual grasses. Includes cheatgrass — fire-cycle changer. |
| ***Lepidium***, ***Cotoneaster***, ***Festuca***, ***Spartina*** | 3 each | Mustards / shrubs / grasses / cordgrasses respectively |
| ***Arctotheca***, ***Avena***, ***Brachypodium***, ***Brassica***, ***Carduus***, ***Carpobrotus***, ***Cirsium***, ***Cortaderia*** | 2 each | Mixed |

When you identify one *Centaurea* or one *Bromus* in the yard, check for siblings — invasive members of these genera often co-occur and share similar management approaches.

## Caveats

- **Cal-IPC PAFs are dated.** Most evaluations are 2003–2008. New introductions (e.g. some grasses, some ornamentals) may not yet be assessed. The "Watch" tier (deferred) catches some of these.
- **No management content here.** Cal-IPC PAFs explicitly omit management info. Removal techniques and reinfestation prevention will be filled in once UC Davis WRIC notes are scraped.
- **"Foothills-relevant" is heuristic.** The Central West + inland-habitat filter catches ~86 plants but may include coastal-leaning species that occasionally appear in inland CW. Refinement requires per-plant body content (and ideally local observations).
- **Limited tier (89 PAFs) and Watch tier (105 PREs) deferred.** The full inventory is 331 plants; this synthesis covers the top 137 only.
