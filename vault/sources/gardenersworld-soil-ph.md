# Soil pH Level Explained — Source Summary

**Author:** BBC Gardeners' World Magazine
**Published:** undated
**URL:** https://www.gardenersworld.com/how-to/grow-plants/soil-ph-level-explained/
**Clipped:** 2026-05-15

## What this source contributes

A focused primer on **soil pH** — what the 0–14 scale means, which plant adaptations match which ranges, how to read mismatch symptoms in plants, and the strong practical recommendation **not to try changing soil pH at scale**.

Three additions to the wiki beyond what existing pages cover:
1. **Plant adaptation vocabulary** — *ericaceous / calcifuge* (acid-loving / lime-hating) vs. *calcioles* (alkaline-loving / lime-tolerant).
2. **Indicator plants** — looking at neighboring landscape plants as a low-friction pH diagnostic before reaching for a test kit (rhodos/camellias/heathers/conifers → acidic; honeysuckle/lilac/clematis/lavender → alkaline).
3. **Mismatch symptoms** — chlorosis (yellow leaves), stunted growth, curled leaves are what a plant tells you when soil pH is wrong for it.

Also reinforces the "amend less, choose better" principle already on [[concepts/soil-basics]], with two operational sub-claims: changing pH at landscape scale isn't worth the trouble (use containers/raised beds for acid-lovers in alkaline regions), and **raising pH (with lime) is easier than lowering it** (with sulfur or ferrous sulfate).

UK context — pH range cited is "most UK soils 4.5–8" which is roughly applicable to California; specific indicator plants are common to both regions.

## Topics covered

1. **The pH scale** — 0–14, neutral=7, acid below, alkaline above.
2. **Plant adaptations** — ericaceous/calcifuge vs. calciole; competitive advantage of specialization.
3. **Mismatch symptoms** — chlorosis (low iron in alkaline soils for acid-lovers; low calcium in acid soils for alkaline-lovers), stunted growth, curling.
4. **Finding pH** — test kit; or read indicator plants growing nearby.
5. **Changing pH** — generally not worth attempting at scale; use containers for off-pH plants; raising pH (lime) is easier than lowering (sulfur).

## Contradictions and corroborations

- **Amend less, choose better** — strongly corroborated. Source goes further: even motivated amenders are advised against attempting landscape-scale pH change. "You'd be better off moving house if you're gardening on chalky alkaline soil and want acid soil."
- **Most plants prefer 6.0–7.0** — corroborated with [[concepts/soil-basics]] existing range.
- **Acidic indicator plants in CA** — partial mapping. Rhododendrons, camellias, conifers (Scot's pine, larch) do indicate acidic soil in CA too. Calcioles (lavender, honeysuckle, lilac) similarly map. *California-native* indicators are absent from this source and would be a useful addition from a CA-native source.
