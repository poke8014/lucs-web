# Design a Garden Layout — Source Summary

**Clipped:** 2026-05-07

## What this source contributes

A beginner-friendly, process-oriented walkthrough of laying out a residential garden *before* picking plants. Generic landscape design — not native-CA-specific, not pollinator-specific. Its value is **planning workflow** that informs the pollinator garden app's site-setup and layout-iteration UX, not its plant data.

Core argument: plant selection is step 9 (last), not step 1. Most beginner gardeners invert this and end up with plants in wrong conditions or wrong places.

## Author's 9-step framework

Recorded here, not promoted to a top-level concept page — this is one designer's framework, kept until corroborated or contradicted by other sources.

1. **Measure your property** — tape measure, site survey, or Google Maps satellite. Capture property lines, structures, home dimensions, gutter/utility/spigot/door/window locations, window sill heights.
2. **Create a [[concepts/site-inventory]]** — map sun, wind, slope, utilities (above and below ground), permanent fixtures, views to highlight/disguise, privacy zones.
3. **Mark primary and secondary paths** — main walkways and traffic flow (shed→garden, porch→utility area).
4. **Place features and focal points** — trees, water features, sculptures, etc. Author marks with "F" for feature, "W" for water feature.
5. **Incorporate must-haves** — patios, dining areas, kids' play space, etc. Iterate Tetris-style.
6. **Fill negative space** — beds, lawn, hardscape. Author offers a [[concepts/softscape-hardscape-ratio]] rule of thumb.
7. **Create paths and flow** — make sure every area is reachable; no dead ends.
8. **Refine the drawing** — optional; what a professional designer would do next (working layout, materials, planting plan).
9. **Select plants** — only after layout is settled.
