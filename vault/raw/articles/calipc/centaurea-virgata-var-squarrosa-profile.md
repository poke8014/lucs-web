## Centaurea virgata ssp. squarrosa

![Centaurea virgata var. squarrosa_squarrose knapweed_JM DiTomaso_cropped](https://www.cal-ipc.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/Centaurea-virgata-var.-squarrosa_squarrose-knapweed_JM-DiTomaso_cropped.jpg)Photo: Joseph DiTomaso

**Synonyms:** Centaurea virgata var. squarrosa, Centaurea squarrosa

**Common names:** squarrose knapweed

_Centaurea virgata_ ssp. _squarrosa_ (squarrose knapweed) is a perennial (family Asteraceae) found in the Klamath and Cascade Ranges, Modoc Plateau and northern Sierra Nevada Mountains of California. Squarrose knapweed favors disturbed open sites, including degraded rangeland, logged areas, grasslands and roadsides. Squarrose knapweed seed dispersal is enhanced by soil and water movement, and by clinging to humans and animals. Seed dispersal and reproduction can be limited by mowing or mechanical removal of plants before seed-set. Squarrose knapweed is a listed noxious weed in several western states. It is native to Western Asia.
