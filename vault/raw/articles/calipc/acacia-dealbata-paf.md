| Section 1: Impact |
| Question 1.1 Impact on abiotic ecosystem processes [?](https://www.cal-ipc.org/plants/paf/acacia-dealbata-plant-assessment-form/#help-111) | BObservational |
| Identify ecosystem processes impacted:<br>Nitrogen fixation and possibly allelopathic litter. Species is fire-adapted but has not appeared to change fire regime any more than current land management practices. Nitrogen-fixing especially detrimental in serpentine areas as it allows other invasives to establish. Some evidence from South Africa that it can alterbank stability in riparian areas.<br> Sources of information:<br>Andrea Williams, observational |
| Question 1.2 Impact on plant community composition,<br>structure, and interactions [?](https://www.cal-ipc.org/plants/paf/acacia-dealbata-plant-assessment-form/#help-121) | BObservational |
| Identify type of impact or alteration:<br>Appears to displace many natives, possibly due to allelopathic effects. This includes Danthonia california, Festuca rubra, Umbellularia california, Ceanothus pumilis, Frangula purshiana and F. californica, Eschscholzia californica, Rhododendron occidentale, etc.<br> Sources of information:<br>Andrea Williams and Peter Warner, observational |

| Identify other regions:<br>Native to the southeastern Australia. In California, it has invaded Del Norte County, near Hiouchi, spreading from old homesites into river bar, grassland, mixed conifer, and Jeffrey pine serpentine woodland. It is also considered invasive in South Africa. Can be found in the bushland in Australia and may invade such areas of California in addition to riparian and coast sites.<br> Sources of information:<br>DiTomaso, J. M., and E. A. Healy. 2007.
