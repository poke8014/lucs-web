## Centaurea stoebe ssp. australis

![Centaurea stoebe ssp. micranthos_ spotted knapweed_Bob Case_cropped](https://www.cal-ipc.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/Centaurea-stoebe-ssp.-micranthos_-spotted-knapweed_Bob-Case_cropped.jpg)Photo: Bob Case

**Synonyms:** Centaurea stoebe ssp. micranthus; Centaurea maculosa; Centaurea biebersteinii

**Common names:** spotted knapweed

_Centaurea stoebe_ subsp. _australis_ (spotted knapweed) is a  biennial or perennial forb (family Asteraceae). A native of Europe and western Asia, it was accidentally introduced into North America in the late 1800s in contaminated alfalfa and clover seed and in soil used for ship ballast. Rosettes and cauline leaves are grey-green and deeply lobed and single-seeded fruits (achenes) have a pappus of uneven bristles. Plants favors disturbed open sites, grasslands, overgrazed rangelands, roadsides and logged areas. Spotted knapweed crowds out native species and forage for livestock, and can invade undisturbed native bunchgrass stands. An individual plant may produce as many as 40,000 seeds. Plants in California are assumed to be the tetraploid “ _australis_” subspecies.
