| Question 2.4 Innate reproductive potential [?](https://www.cal-ipc.org/plants/paf/schinus-terebinthifolius-plant-assessment-form/#help-241) | BOther Published Material |
| Describe key reproductive characteristics:<br>Mostly dioecious with male and female flowers on separate trees. Reproduces by seed. Flowering begins in September, and by mid-October most trees are in flower. Most flowering ceases in November. Fruit ripening happens soon after flowering. Mature female trees are prolific seed producers, which, combined with viability rates of 30-60%, results in many seedlings. Ripe fruits are retained on the tree for up to eight months. Seeds lose viability approximately 5 months after dispersal. Most seed germination occurs in January and February. Survival of seedlings ranges from 66-100%. Begins reproducing within 3 years of germination. Can resprout from aboveground stems and root crowns following cutting, girdling, or fire. Its shallow root system allows for development of suckers that produce another plant.<br> Sources of information:<br>Randall 2000<br>DiTomaso and Healy. 2006. Weeds of California. UC DANR Publ. #3488. |

| Identify dispersal mechanisms:<br>Popular as an ornamental, but not as popular in California as S. molle. The original route of introduction into California was through the nursery industry. Occasionally escapes cultivation from gardens.<br> Sources of information:<br>Randall 2000 |

Raccoons and opossums deposit seeds with fecal material, providing nutrients for the germinating seed (1). Relies on frugivores for dispersal (2).
