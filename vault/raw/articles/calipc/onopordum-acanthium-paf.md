| Question 3.1 Ecological amplitude/Range [?](https://www.cal-ipc.org/plants/paf/onopordum-acanthium-plant-assessment-form/#help-311) | BOther Published Material |
| Scotch Thistle has a wide range of elevations in North America where it has been found, from 200 feet to 7,000 feet. Inhabits wet meadows, pastures, small grain fields, dry alfalfa, as well as disturbed areas in open rangeland. Grows best on the slope between arid rangeland and wet meadows along streams. Observed to invade undisturbed sage brush areas. The first mention of Scotch Thistle in CA in the literature is an article by T.C. Fuller in 1958, stating that Modoc County Ag Comm submitted the first sample for identification in 1957. The major area of infestation at the time in Modoc County was the Hung Estate Ranch which had 500 - 600 acres. According to that reprt, Scotch thistle was present at that location in 1942. In 1958, plants were found in Lassen and Lake Counties. Earliest reference to this plant being in the US is the 5th edition of Grays Manual dated 1878. Infests disturbed and neglected lands. It prefers sites near ditch banks and rivers, but also infests pastureland, crops, rangeland, and roadsides.<br> Sources of information:<br>Draft Action Plan for Scotch Thistle Eradication in Modoc and Lassen Counties, 1994. CDFA-IPC internal document from files at Redding Field Office.<br>Kadrmas, T. and W.S. Johnson. Managing Scotch Thistle. University of Nevada, Reno, Cooperative Extension. Fact Sheet FS-02-57, <>. |

#### Related traits:

A single plant can produce over 30,000 seeds, which have a high degree of dormancy. Scotch thistle can flower and produce seed at less than 12 inches tall. Scotch thistle reproduces only by seed.
