Have seen it worse in Southern Oregon, same habitat (QUGA4 woodlands), they even have a white oak/hedgehog dogtail association in their forest mapping (2).<br> Sources of information:<br>1\. | Identify dispersal mechanisms:<br>Possibly some movement in hayfields, but this is not a common weed in areas with hay production.<br> Sources of information:<br>DiTomaso, observational. |
| Question 2.6 Potential for natural long-distance dispersal [?](https://www.cal-ipc.org/plants/paf/cynosurus-echinatus-plant-assessment-form/#help-261) | BOther Published Material |
| Identify dispersal mechanisms:<br>Awned seeds facilitate long distance transport by animals, but most seed probably drop directly beneath parent plant. Fertile florets fall near the parent plant and probably disperse to greater distances with water, mud, and by clinging to animals, vehicle tires, and human shoes and clothing. A persistent seedbank does not appear to develop.<br> Sources of information:<br>DiTomaso and Healy. 2006. Weeds of California. UC DANR Publ. #3488. |

| Identify other regions:<br>Native to Europe. One of the most invasive species of Oregon oak woodlands in British Columbia. Also occurs in Oregon, Washington, many eastern and southern states, and a few south-central states.<br> Sources of information:<br>1\. DiTomaso and Healy in prep. |

Inhabits grassland, chaparral, oak woodland, summer dry pasture, coastal bluffs and terraces, riverbanks, and other disturbed places (1).Very common along the coast, and even more so inland. A major understory component in oak woodlands (so is somewhat shade-tolerant), and in almost all grasslands in the N. Coast Ranges and along the coast (2). Roadsides, fields, grassland, chaparral, oak woodland, summer dry pasture, coastal bluffs and terraces, riverbanks, other disturbed places.<br> Sources of information:<br>1\.
