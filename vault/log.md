Notable findings / deltas:
- **Mallows**: glyphosate doesn't control. UC IPM explicitly notes mallow is one of the few weeds where glyphosate fails — surface this in user-facing copy.
- **Field bindweed**: 20-ft root depth; **60-year seedbank**; complete landscape-fabric kill takes 3–5 years. A different-magnitude problem than the average annual weed.
- **Dandelion**: no true stems → mowing accomplishes nothing. Persistent grubbing of seedlings is the only mechanical control.
- **Dallisgrass**: UC IPM's pragmatic recommendation is **complete lawn renovation** for severe infestations — selective postemergent products are licensed-applicator-only and require 3 applications over 2–3 years.
- **Kikuyugrass**: once it reaches 40% of a lawn, UC IPM advises **maintaining it as the turf species** rather than fighting it. Hand-cultivation breaks rhizomes into propagules — particularly when followed by irrigation.
- **Common knotweed**: a soil-compaction indicator. Fixing the underlying compaction is the upstream control move.
- **Plantains**: like dandelion, grow from a crown below the mower blade. Broadleaf plantain signals compacted alkaline low-N soil; buckhorn signals dry low-fertility.
- **Common purslane**: succulent stems re-root from cultivation — hoeing-then-irrigation effectively transplants. Disposal rule: dry on hard surface or bag, don't drop on damp soil.
- **Russian thistle**: thrives in disturbed loose soil — well-managed gardens essentially exempt. Tumbleweed accumulation is a serious fire hazard.
- **Spotted spurge**: 5-week germination-to-seed cycle; milky sap is a skin/eye irritant. Both surface in user-facing notes.
- **Field dodder vs. Japanese dodder**: native dodder species (*C. pentagona* and relatives) get hand-control; **Japanese dodder is under California eradication program** — don't self-treat, call county Ag Commissioner. The page distinguishes both clearly.
- **Pacific poison-oak**: only native in the new set. Included per [[feedback-sunshower-weed-scope]] — homeowners routinely remove for safety despite ecological value. Carries `nativity: native`. Specific guidance on urushiol washing technique (forceful unidirectional dish-soap method within 10 minutes), don't-burn-don't-mow rules, and `pull_vine_dig_crown` default method.
- **Burning + stinging nettles**: one page covers both — *U. urens* (annual non-native) and *U. dioica* (native perennial). Stinging nettle is host to native butterflies; only remove where safety dictates.
- **Spread pattern surfaces:** 5 weeds in this rollout that compete primarily in disturbed soil (Russian thistle, puncturevine, knotweed, dodder) all have UC IPM management notes that say "fix the underlying condition" (compaction, bare soil, disturbance) as the upstream lever. This is a recurring pattern worth surfacing in a future concepts page (residential cleanup-plan concept: "disturbed-soil weeds — what they're telling you").
