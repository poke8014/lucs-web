# Composting

Composting turns kitchen and garden waste into a soil amendment over roughly a year. The finished compost is used as a **mulch** around the base of plants and in the veg patch.

From [[sources/gardenersworld-beginner-tips]]:

> Composting kitchen and garden waste is good for the environment, wildlife, your purse and also your garden. Let the waste break down for a year and then use it as a mulch around the base of plants in your garden and veg patch.

The source treats composting at a one-paragraph level; the canonical beginner detail (carbon-to-nitrogen ratio, turning, moisture, browns/greens) is not covered here. A future deeper source will fill this in.

## Caveat for California native gardens

Compost as mulch around **California natives** is more nuanced than for generic ornamentals:

- Many CA natives evolved on lean soils and can be harmed by rich compost mulches that change soil chemistry and favor weedy generalists.
- **Inorganic mulches** (gravel, decomposed granite) often suit native plantings better than rich organic mulch — especially for chaparral and sage-scrub species.
- Compost still makes sense around vegetable beds, fruit trees, and most non-native ornamentals.

This caveat parallels the broader pattern flagged on [[concepts/soil-basics]] and [[concepts/watering]] — generic temperate horticulture advice (1"/week, amend with compost, feed regularly) doesn't transfer cleanly to native CA practice.
