# Vegetative Spread

Many invasive plants spread without seeds — they propagate by stem, root, or tissue fragments instead. Their management is fundamentally different from seed-spreaders, and standard removal techniques (mowing, hand-pulling) often **make the problem worse** rather than better.

## Mechanisms

| Mechanism | What it is | Example |
|-----------|------------|---------|
| **Stolons** | Above-ground horizontal stems that root at nodes | [[plants/arctotheca-prostrata]] |
| **Rhizomes** | Below-ground horizontal stems | (to be filled in as plants ingested — Arundo donax, Spartina spp. expected) |
| **Fragmentation** | Broken plant pieces re-root and establish | [[plants/arctotheca-prostrata]] |
| **Resprouting** | Plant regrows from cut stumps, severed roots, or burned tissue | (to be filled in) |
| **Layering** | Branches root where they touch the ground | (to be filled in) |

A plant may use multiple mechanisms — capeweed spreads by stolons, fragments easily, *and* resprouts when cut.

## Why standard removal often fails

A vegetative spreader broken into many pieces produces **many new plants**, not zero plants:

- **Mowing fragments and disperses tissue.** For capeweed, mowing along roadsides directly facilitates spread (PAF Q2.3). For many rhizome-spreaders, mechanical disturbance breaks rhizomes and each fragment establishes elsewhere.
- **Hand-pulling rosettes leaves rooted stolons behind.** The visible plant comes up; the stolons remain anchored and re-leaf.
- **Composting clippings can spread the plant.** Home compost piles rarely reach temperatures high enough to kill viable rhizome and stolon tissue. **Dispose of vegetative-spreader clippings in green-waste landfill or burn**, not compost.
- **Tilled or disturbed soil spreads root fragments.** Rototilling a rhizome-infested area distributes hundreds of new propagules across the bed.

## Implications for management

- **Plan for repeated visits.** Vegetative-spreader removal is rarely one-and-done. Schedule monitoring and follow-up over multiple seasons.
- **Capture every fragment.** Tarp the work area; collect rosettes, stolons, and roots in bags; hand-dig where possible.
- **Avoid mechanical methods that fragment.** Mowing, weed-whacking, tilling all spread these plants. Sheet-mulching, smothering, or careful hand removal are usually better first lines.
- **Watch the disposal chain.** Don't compost. Don't leave piles where they can re-root.

## Why this matters for the wiki

In the [[sources/calipc]] PAFs, the **Worksheet A** answers "Has quickly spreading vegetative structures (rhizomes, roots, etc.)" / "Fragments easily and fragments can become established elsewhere" / "Resprouts readily when cut, grazed, or burned" identify the plants in this category. Plant pages capture this in `invasive.spread_mechanisms` frontmatter (`stolons`, `rhizomes`, `fragments`, `resprouts`, etc.).

When [[concepts/right-plant-right-place]] goes wrong with a vegetative spreader, the cleanup cost compounds — this is one reason Phase 1 yard prep can dominate the early calendar of a native garden plan.
