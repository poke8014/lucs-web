# Management, Not Maintenance

Principle 5 of a [[concepts/designed-plant-communities|designed plant community]], and the whole of Ch. 4: once plants are matched to the site and each other, you stop **maintaining individuals** and start **managing the whole community** toward design goals. From Rainer & West's [[sources/planting-in-a-post-wild-world|*Planting in a Post-Wild World*]].

## Maintenance vs. management

| Traditional maintenance | Community management |
|---|---|
| Props up individual plants (water, fertilize, deadhead, mulch) | Steers the whole planting with large-scale actions (mow, burn, selective edit/add) |
| Fixed schedule regardless of need | Goal-driven — intervene only to hold design intent |
| Freezes plants at a moment in time | Allows change: plants self-seed, move, compete, die and get replaced by neighbors |
| Aims to keep one plant alive | Aims to preserve the community's structure and balance |

The manager works "more like a referee than a prison guard" — small corrections of the rudder, only when the planting drifts off its design goals (ground going bare, an aggressive plant swamping a demure one, legibility eroding). Left entirely alone most communities thrive but eventually shift enough to lose the original intent; management keeps them on course. It requires **humility** — recognizing the planting is a complex adaptive system you steer, not control.

This is the design-theory root of the [[concepts/wildlife-coexistence|"leave the stems standing / don't over-tidy"]] direction Sunshower already takes, and it's the low-input promise that makes native gardens realistic for "over-committed homeowners" (a client type the authors name explicitly).

## The three establishment phases

Management goals shift over a planting's life. The phases are a *gradient*, not clean stages — disturbance (a tree falls, deer eat a patch) can reset part of a planting to the start.

### 1. Plant establishment phase
From installation until every plant is rooted in and self-supporting (weeks to months). **Goal: 100% survival and fast ground cover** — beat the weeds to the open soil. Threats: erosion, plant loss, weed invasion.
- Slow structural/taprooted plants (Baptisia, milkweed) put energy *underground* first and look like they're "just sitting there" — this is normal, not failure; don't fertilize or replace in a panic.
- Cutting plants back early channels energy into crowns/roots (but not too low, and not on evergreen sedges before frost).
- Keep herbivores (deer, rabbits) off tender un-hardened foliage.
- **When a plant dies, diagnose why** — it's site feedback. A cool wet spring favoring cool-season growers over warm-season grasses may mask a *good* selection thwarted by weather; note what thrives to guide future planting.

### 2. Landscape establishment phase
From "all plants self-supporting" until the planting reaches mature form — ground covers close the soil, structure gains height, seasonal themes strengthen. **Goal: let the design emerge and fix mistakes now** (flopping structural plants, spotty ground cover). Plant-vs-plant competition begins; fillers and cover crops decline as stronger species dominate. Species that fail *now* are genuinely unfit for the site.

### 3. Post-establishment phase
The rest of the planting's life. **Goal: creative management** — keep it legible, functional, and balanced through selective, occasional intervention; allow ongoing change within the design's bounds.

## Site prep sets this up (the pre-work)

Management is easier when installation was done right — Ch. 4's prep guidance overlaps heavily with the cleanup-plan:
- **Limit disturbance.** Every disturbed patch is a weed opportunity; cover bare soil fast (seed, cover crop, temporary mulch). Fence off zones to avoid [[concepts/soil-basics|compaction]].
- **Clear thoroughly before planting.** Remove weeds *including* roots/storage organs; target each weed at its life-cycle weak point; expect a full season (or two) on a badly infested site. This is the same logic as [[synthesis/invasive-removal-methods]] and [[concepts/vegetative-spread]].
- **Treat compaction** with deep plowing / sub-soiling / core aeration rather than tilling (tilling collapses pores, destroys soil biology, and surfaces buried weed seed). A diverse-rooted community then keeps loosening the soil itself.
- **Density is the weed defense.** The through-line: a fully covered ground (green mulch) is the durable, low-labor answer to reinfestation — filling the niche denies it to weeds.

## Why this matters for Sunshower

- The three phases are the natural spine for the **saved-plans outcome log** — a plan isn't "done" at install; it moves through establishment, and the log should prompt phase-appropriate check-ins ("Year 1: is ground cover closing? which plants died, and why?"). See [[synthesis/post-wild-world-and-sunshower]].
- "Diagnose deaths as site feedback" is a concrete, high-value logging behavior — it turns a failed plant into RPRP data for the next section.
- Reinforces the cleanup-plan's existing message that Phase-1 prep dominates the early calendar and pays off later.
