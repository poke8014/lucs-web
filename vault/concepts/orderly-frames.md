# Orderly Frames

The technique for making an ecologically rich — and therefore visually "messy" — [[concepts/designed-plant-communities|designed plant community]] read as *intentional* rather than *neglected*. This is principle 4 (make it attractive and legible) made concrete. From Rainer & West's [[sources/planting-in-a-post-wild-world|*Planting in a Post-Wild World*]] (Ch. 1 & 3), building on Joan Iverson Nassauer's essay *"Messy Ecosystems, Orderly Frames"* (1988).

## The problem

What is good ecologically (biodiverse, layered, dynamic) often looks disorderly, and what looks tidy (mown lawn, clipped hedges) is often ecologically poor and unsustainable. The public reads illegible plantings as abandoned lots or urban decay. A young mixed planting is *especially* vulnerable — before it fills in, it lacks structure and flowers and is hard to tell from a weed patch, which can trigger a "vicious cycle of disinvestment." Our preference for tidy, long-view, savanna-like landscapes is partly a 18th-century picturesque inheritance and partly innate.

## The two moves

### 1. Stylize the planting itself
A designed community should be a **distillation** of a wild one, not a replica: emphasize its essential layers and patterns, exaggerate the signature drifts, delete some elements, set random mixes against strong massed blocks. Amplifying the readable patterns makes the planting "more than the sum of its parts." Keep **legibility in the design layers** even while packing diversity into the [[concepts/planting-layers|functional layers]].

### 2. Give it an orderly frame — "cues to care"
Surround the loose planting with signals of human intention and maintenance (Nassauer's "cues to care"):

- **Mown lawn verge** around a meadow (lawn and beds improve each other; you don't have to replace all the lawn).
- **Clipped hedges, walls, fences, cobble edging** — architectural frames.
- **Paths** (mown strips, mulch, hardscape) that double as maintenance access and a crisp edge.
- **Restrained height** — keep most planting below waist/chest height (18–30") so people can look *over* it; use only occasional see-through tall accents (leafless upper stems).
- **A planted frame** — a neat band of low, tidy species (or a mown outer strip) around a taller mixed interior.
- **Clean, densely planted edges**, since edges communicate the level of care most loudly.

## Why this matters for Sunshower

- Orderly frames are the mechanism that lets a **naturalistic** density style stay socially acceptable in a suburban front yard — it's the answer to "won't the neighbors think it's overgrown?" The [[concepts/wildlife-coexistence|friendly-to-natives tone]] and this legibility toolkit are what make a native/naturalistic garden *defensible* to an HOA or a skeptical spouse.
- The landscaped ↔ naturalistic slider is essentially a **frame-strength slider**: more framing + more stylization = "landscaped"; looser + more intermingling = "naturalistic." Same plants, different framing. See [[synthesis/post-wild-world-and-sunshower]].
- "Cues to care" is encodable as concrete plan advice (add a mown edge, keep a tidy front band, define the bed with a hard line) — a checker-style tip, not a generator.
