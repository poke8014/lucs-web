# Softscape / Hardscape Ratio

A rule of thumb for residential garden layouts: roughly **1/3 softscape** (planting beds, garden beds) to **2/3 open space** (lawn, patios, pathways, hardscape).

## Definitions

- **Softscape** — living, planted areas: garden beds, perennial borders, shrub massings.
- **Hardscape** — built/paved areas: patios, walkways, walls, decks.
- **Open space** in this ratio includes both hardscape *and* unplanted but uncluttered ground (lawn).

## Why it matters

It's a heuristic for not over- or under-planting. A yard that's 90% beds reads as overwhelming and is exhausting to maintain. A yard that's 5% beds reads as a parking lot.

The author of [[sources/design-a-garden-layout]] is explicit that this is **not a hard rule** — gardeners who love planting (and gardens with specific design goals) often exceed the 1/3 share. It's a starting point for the [[concepts/bubble-drawing]] negative-space step, not a constraint.

## Caveats and open questions

- This ratio comes from a generic residential-design source. **Pollinator gardens may justify a higher softscape share** by design — the goal is forage and habitat, not lawn-and-patio living. When a pollinator-specific source addresses this, this page should be updated or contradicted.
- The ratio doesn't distinguish maintained lawn from meadow plantings, or between gravel hardscape and decking. Texture and plant density matter at least as much as raw ratio.
- No source yet on whether this ratio applies the same way at small scales (e.g., a 200 sq ft urban yard) as at suburban scales.
