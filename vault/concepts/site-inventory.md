# Site Inventory

A **site inventory** is the systematic mapping of a property's physical conditions before any layout or plant decisions are made. It produces an annotated drawing of the yard recording sun, wind, slope, utilities, fixed structures, sightlines, and noise/privacy zones.

The site inventory is the prerequisite to layout design. Conditions captured here drive *every downstream decision*: where a patio goes, where a screening hedge is needed, which beds get sun-loving plants vs. shade plants, which corners need terracing.

## Why it matters

The site inventory is the input side of [[concepts/right-plant-right-place]]. Most beginners pick plants first and end up putting sun-lovers in shade or moisture-lovers on a dry slope. A site inventory inverts the order: **map conditions → match conditions to plants/features**, not the reverse. It also turns "flaws" of a property (slope, awkward sightline, road noise) into design constraints you can solve, rather than ignore.

For a pollinator garden specifically, the inventory determines which microclimates exist on the property — and therefore which native plant communities will actually thrive where.

## What to capture

### Physical structures and fixtures
- Property lines and existing structures (house, garage, shed)
- Doors, windows (and their height from ground — don't block them with oversized plants)
- Permanent fixtures: AC units, spigots, outlets, meters, gutters
- Common walking paths (mark with arrows)

### Sun and aspect
- **Sun map** — which areas of the yard get how much direct sun, and at what time of day. See [[concepts/sun-requirements]] for the threshold definitions and what "full / part / shade" mean on the plant side.
- **North arrow** — establishes the property's *aspect* (which direction it faces). South-facing slopes warm earlier; north-facing stay cooler/moister.

### Wind
- Prevailing wind direction. In the US, prevailing winds typically blow west→east (worth verifying for your region — coastal CA has its own patterns).
- Wind matters for where to put delicate plants, screening, outdoor dining areas.

### Utilities (above and below ground)
- Overhead electrical lines — don't plant tall trees beneath them.
- Underground electrical, water, sewer — call **811** in the US to have utilities marked before digging.

### Topography
- Grade and slope changes — where water pools, where it drains, where you'd need [[concepts/terracing|terracing]] (page not yet created).

### Sightlines and privacy
- Views to **highlight** (a neighbor's mature oak from your kitchen window).
- Views to **disguise** (a junk pile, an HVAC unit, a road).
- Privacy zones — near outdoor dining, against an overlooking second-story neighbor, etc.

## Workflow

The inventory is a drawing, not a list. Make a measured base plan first, then layer the inventory on top with notes, arrows, and zones. Make several copies — the inventory feeds the [[concepts/bubble-drawing]] step that follows, and you'll want fresh copies for each layout iteration.

## Relationship to other concepts

- Feeds [[concepts/right-plant-right-place]] — the inventory is the input side; plant selection is the output side.
- Drives the [[concepts/bubble-drawing]] iteration step — bubble drawings layer features and beds *on top of* the site inventory.
- Sun-map data flows into [[concepts/sun-requirements]] for the plant-side match.
- Soil observations during the inventory feed [[concepts/soil-basics]].
- Slope/grade observations may surface a need for terracing or rain-garden placement.
- Microclimate observations (south-facing walls, frost pockets at slope bottoms) are inputs to [[concepts/frost-and-cold-protection]] for "delicate" plants on the edge of [[concepts/usda-hardiness-zones|zone tolerance]].
